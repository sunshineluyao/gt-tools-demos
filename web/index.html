REVENUE EQUIVALENCE · MONTE CARLO CHECK

Same benchmark allocation, same expected revenue

With symmetric risk-neutral bidders, independent private values, no reserve, and identical treatment of the lowest type, the formats converge in expectation—not in every realized auction.

BEYOND THE BENCHMARK

Which assumption breaks first?

Each stress test changes the game or the institution. Reclassify before importing an equilibrium result.

ECONOMICS

Predict and evaluate

COMPUTER SCIENCE

BEHAVIORAL SCIENCE

Compare actual bids with the benchmark; study risk, learning, framing, comprehension, and bounded reasoning without overgeneralizing classroom evidence.

03STRESS-TEST

Interdisciplinary Contribution

Where, exactly, does your work change knowledge?

A strong project does not place economics, computer science, and behavioral science beside one another. It makes their evidence streams change one shared claim.

QResearch questionNew phenomenon, tension, or causal target?
EEconomic modelNew incentives, welfare criterion, or institution?
CComputational methodNew algorithm, environment, or benchmark?
BBehavioral evidenceNew assumptions, response data, or heterogeneity?
AApplicationNew setting, population, or implementation?
VValidationNew comparison, robustness test, or boundary?
ΣSynthesisDoes integration change the explanation?

Score each source 0–3: 0 absent · 1 adjacent · 2 material · 3 indispensable. Award “synthesis” only when removing one discipline would change the shared question, method, inference, or application—not merely shorten the reference list.

ABSTRACT GAP CHECK

Make sentence two do real work.

Sentence 1 establishes the problem. Sentence 2 must begin with However or Yet and identify a specific limitation in existing knowledge. Only then present your approach and result.

GENERATIVE-AI LITERATURE STRESS TEST

Try to disprove your novelty claim.

Generative AI may retrieve candidates and propose comparisons; it cannot certify novelty. A human must open the paper, verify metadata, inspect the relevant passage, and revise the claim.

  1. OBSERVEWrite your contribution claim before prompting.
  2. SEARCHAsk for the closest papers across question, method, and application.
  3. VERIFYOpen DOI or publisher pages; record exact evidence and uncertainty.
  4. REVISENarrow, relocate, or withdraw any claim that does not survive.
  5. DISCLOSERecord the model, date, prompt purpose, and human decisions.

G
Who chooses what, and with which consequences?
Objects: students and schools; reported rankings and acceptance rules; payoff or ordinal preference over assignments.
Test: hold everyone else’s strategy fixed. Can one participant gain by changing only their report?
Strategic thinking makes the incentive problem visible.

        
S
What should a collective assignment accomplish?
Objects: welfare, access, fairness, efficiency, priority respect, and legitimacy.
Test: compare outcomes under explicit social criteria and disclose conflicts among those criteria.
Social choice makes the value judgment visible.

        
M
Which rules implement those goals under strategic behavior?
Objects: allocation algorithm, reports, priorities, capacity, stability, feasibility, and strategy-proofness.
Test: prove or falsify each property; do not infer it from a favorable example.
Mechanism design makes the institution—and its constraints—visible.
